How this works

  • Long-term contract mode applies your entered $/GW rate uniformly to the entire revenue-generating GW base each quarter — a stable, blended contract price, same mechanic the underlying model uses.
  • Spot market mode treats your entered rate as the price new capacity is sold at when it comes online that quarter. Each quarter's incremental GW becomes its own "vintage," and its price glides linearly toward the long-term steady-state rate over your chosen convergence window (3–5 years). The blended rate you see is the GW-weighted average across all vintages live that quarter — it can stay above or below the long-term rate for a while if new, richly-priced capacity keeps arriving faster than old vintages converge.
  • Space, Connectivity, AI cost ratios, D&A, financing and below-the-line items hold at the model's base-case schedule and are not user-editable here.
  • Depreciation on new capex starts 2 quarters after spend (build/ramp lag) and runs at 0.7×(1/20)+0.3×(1/56) of that quarter's total capex per quarter thereafter (70% IT equipment over 5 years, 30% facility over 14 years), consistent with the source model.
  • Cash, debt and interest are a full roll-forward: new debt draws automatically whenever cash would fall under the $15,000mm minimum buffer, at 6.5%/yr; existing $39,364mm of debt accrues at 5.5%/yr; cash & securities earn 4%/yr.
  • The balance sheet holds "other assets" and "other liabilities" flat at their Jun 30, 2026 actual level (no working-capital or deferred-revenue modeling) and solves equity as the balancing plug; the memo row below it shows how much of the equity movement is net income versus other reconciling items (chiefly the model's $3,681mm/quarter legacy non-cash add-back, which increases cash but isn't charged against forecast-period net income).
  • This is an illustrative business-case model, not SpaceX guidance or analyst consensus. All forecast figures are scenario outputs of the inputs you set above.
